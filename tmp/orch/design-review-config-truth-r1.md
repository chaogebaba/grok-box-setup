### JUDGED (design reasoning)
- The three BLOCKERs are all "the cited precedent does not fit the shape the decision assumes," which forces a builder to invent the actual mechanism — the definition of a non-zero-decision wall. B1 (integration point) and B2 (transport+write shape) are the load-bearing ones; B3 is the safety corner (truncated stdin) the failure-mode section (prompt item 5) explicitly asked to be covered and the wall does not.
- Precedent-first is otherwise strong: this blueprint is unusually good at citing real fleetctl/boxup patterns (canary/abort, state files, notify, awk grammar). The greenfield managed.toml/fleet.toml layer is genuinely new and correctly flagged as such in the blueprint's own header.
- Invariant safety: managed.toml as a SEPARATE file (D1) is the right call — it preserves the "config.toml is the user's" DESIGN.md invariant untouched and inherits the /workspace image-swap-survival property by placement. Secrets: [ssh].password in managed.toml at 600 owner root is consistent with config.toml's handling; the only gap is the transient VPS-side render scratch (N1).
- The M-series are coherence gaps between D8's box-side opt-out and D10's "brain reports what it pushed" — the brain's in-sync record can diverge from the box's effective config, and the operator diff tool (D7) as specified cannot see the divergence (M4/N2). Not fatal, but a builder will produce a misleading `config diff` unless told.

**Zero-decision buildable: NO** — resolve B1 (row-e integration point), B2 (D5 transport is `tunnel_ssh` + whole-file writer, not the tailnet `box_ssh` transform), and B3 (truncated-stdin safety) before the empirical gate. The four MAJORs are drafted amendments; fold or record skip rationale.
